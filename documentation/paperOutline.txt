Outline for a numerical methods paper on the monolithic beam + rigid body solver
================================================================================

This is a suggested layout for writing up the monolithic beam + rigid body
coupling as a paper for a numerical methods journal (for example Int. J.
Numer. Meth. Engng, Comput. Methods Appl. Mech. Engrg, J. Comput. Phys.,
Computers & Structures). For each section it says what should go in it,
and which file in this folder already holds the material.

needs a source checked by hand before it goes in.


Working title
-------------

  "A monolithic Newton-Raphson coupling of a finite-volume geometrically
   exact beam and a rigid body, for moored floating bodies"

Alternatives: "Monolithic finite-volume beam-rigid body coupling with
Newmark integration in displacement form"; "Removing the partitioned
stability limit in beam-moored rigid body simulations".


Abstract (150-250 words)
------------------------

  - Problem: a floating rigid body moored by flexible lines, inside a CFD
    simulation. The body and lines are usually coupled by partitioned
    iteration.
  - Difficulty: partitioned coupling becomes unstable for light bodies and
    stiff, short lines (the limit beta dt^2 EA/(L m_pin) > ~1).
  - Method: the body's Newton-Euler equations, integrated with Newmark-beta
    in displacement form, are added as 6 extra rows and columns to the
    finite-volume beam's Newton system. The joint compatibility is
    eliminated, so beam and body are advanced together by one Newton
    iteration.
  - Results: agrees with analytic and partitioned reference solutions;
    second-order in time; stable where the partitioned scheme diverges;
    one PIMPLE outer corrector is enough, which reduces cost.
  - Implementation in OpenFOAM (beamFoam / moorFV), with a single switch
    between partitioned and monolithic coupling.


1. Introduction
---------------

  - Motivation: floating offshore structures (wave energy converters,
    floating wind, aquaculture) moored by lines that bend, twist and go
    slack. CFD of the body needs a mooring model that is both accurate and
    robust.
  - Mooring models: quasi-static, lumped-mass (e.g. MoorDyn), finite
    element and finite volume beam models.
  - Coupling: partitioned (body solver and line solver take turns) versus
    monolithic (one system). Partitioned stability problems for light
    bodies and stiff connections (added-mass-like instability).
  - Gap: no finite-volume geometrically exact beam with the rigid body in
    the same Newton system (check this claim against the literature).
  - Contributions, as a numbered list:
      1. Monolithic formulation: body residuals in force units, Newmark-beta
         in displacement form, rotations by a step rotation vector.
      2. Elimination of the joint (pin and welded) so the attachment
         displacement is not an unknown.
      3. Block structure of the coupled Jacobian, and its physical
         interpretation (action-reaction in the columns, non-symmetry).
      4. Stability analysis of the partitioned scheme on a linear model,
         giving the limit the monolithic scheme removes.
      5. Verification and application to moored floating bodies in waves.
  - Paper layout paragraph.


2. Governing equations
----------------------

2.1 Geometrically exact beam
  - Total Lagrangian, Simo-Reissner type: displacement W, rotation
    tensor Lambda (incremental rotation Theta), internal force Q and
    moment M.
  - Balance of linear and angular momentum per unit length, including the
    lever-arm term R' x Q.
  - Constitutive law: CQ = diag(EA, GA, GA), CM = diag(GJ, EIyy, EIzz) in
    section axes. J is the torsion constant (pi R^4/2 for a solid circle).
  - Loads: gravity, distributed hydrodynamic load (if any), ground contact.
  Sources: ML section 9 (coefficient origins), TC "What each symbol is".

2.2 Rigid body
  - Newton-Euler about the centre of mass: m a + c v = F_ext + F_beam,
    J alpha + omega x J omega + c_r omega = M_ext + r x F_beam (+ M_beam).
  - Orientation R = exp(theta) R_0, inertia J = R J_body R^T.
  - Fluid force and moment from the CFD as external loads; motion
    constraints (e.g. surge-heave-pitch only) as projections.
  Sources: ML 9.4, CP section 4.

2.3 Joint between beam end and body
  - Compatibility: attachment point u_b = x + (R - I) r_0.
  - Pin: force passes, no moment (zero-moment end condition).
  - Welded: end section rotates with the body; force and moment pass.
  - Action-reaction: the body receives -Q_b (and -M_b if welded).
  Sources: TC (layout, B_b), ML section 6.


3. Discretisation
-----------------

3.1 Finite-volume beam in space
  - Cell-centred unknowns, face fluxes of force and moment.
  - Newton linearisation of the face force and moment: the face
    coefficients k, kappa, g, gamma, K^M, eta (definitions and physical
    meaning). Boundary faces, half-cell distance pDelta.
  - Pin end factor B = (kappa + gamma)^-1 kappa: the end face rotation
    eliminated by the zero-moment condition.
  Sources: ML sections 9.1-9.3, TC "What each symbol is" and "Notation".

3.2 Time integration
  - Beam: Newmark (as implemented) with its predictor.
  - Body: Newmark-beta in displacement form. Predictor
        x~ = x_n + dt v_n + dt^2 (1/2 - beta) a_n,
        a_{n+1} = (x_{n+1} - x~)/(beta dt^2),
    and the same for the step rotation vector theta. Explain why the
    predictor has no beta dt^2 a_{n+1} term.
  - Rotation Jacobian T(theta) and its closed form.
  Sources: ML sections 9.4 and 10, NB sections 1 and 1a.


4. Monolithic coupling
----------------------

  The core of the paper.

4.1 Unknowns and residuals
  - [DW, DTheta per cell; dx, dtheta for the body], 6 x 6 blocks.
  - Body residuals in force units:
        R_x  = m (x - x~)/(beta dt^2) + c v - F_ext + Q_b
        R_th = J alpha + omega x J omega + c_r omega - M_ext + r x Q_b
  - Statement and short proof that a converged Newton iterate satisfies
    the Newmark-beta update exactly (the linearisation of Q_b is the only
    approximation per iteration).
  Sources: NB sections 2-4.

4.2 Eliminating the joint
  - du_b = dx - A dtheta, A = [r]x T.
  - How the attachment-face source term K_b (W_b - W_b,prev) becomes the
    body columns of the matrix.
  Sources: MP section 2, ML section 6.

4.3 Linearisation and block structure
  - The coupling blocks (table), and the body diagonal including the
    turning-arm term [Q_b]x A and the gyroscopic terms.
  - Physical reading: every coupling entry is the linearised face force
    dQ_b passed through a row operator (I, I, lever arm K^M, [r]x).
    Action-reaction appears down the columns, not across transposes.
  - Why the Jacobian is not symmetric even with consistent signs.
  - Pin versus welded: extra blocks for the welded joint.
  - Motion constraints as projections of the body rows and columns.
  Sources: ML sections 1-8, TC "Where the action-reaction balance is".

4.4 Solution of the linear system
  - Assembly into one sparse matrix; sparse LU (non-symmetric).
  - Sparsity: block tridiagonal beam plus one bordering block row and
    column at the attachment cell. Cost relative to the beam alone.
  Sources: ML sections 3-4, CP sections 5-6.

4.5 Algorithm
  - Pseudo-code for one time step: new time step (store state, reset
    theta), Newton loop (assemble beam, add body blocks, solve, update
    beam and body, check convergence), accept (update v, a, omega, alpha).
  Sources: CP, NB section 3.


5. The partitioned scheme and its stability limit
-------------------------------------------------

  - The partitioned algorithm used for comparison (FvBeamNewmark): body
    Newmark step against the frozen beam force, then the beam with the
    end prescribed, repeated in the outer correctors.
  - Linear body-spring model: amplification matrices for the partitioned
    loop and the monolithic update. Show the partitioned iteration
    diverges when beta dt^2 k/m_pin > ~1, with
    m_pin = 1/(1/m + a^2/J) for an off-centre attachment.
  - One outer corrector: partitioned is explicit, first order and
    unstable; monolithic is implicit and second order.
  Sources: OC (figures growth.png, newton.png), source/model.py,
  source/order.py, memory notes on the partitioned limit, the
  rigidBodyEndWhirl sweeps.

7. Verification
---------------

  Each test: setup, what it checks, the result, a figure or table.
  - Translation only: body on a beam spring, against an analytic
    spring-mass solution.
  - Rotation: off-centre attachment and twist (rigidBodyEndTwist), pin
    and welded joints.
  - Order of accuracy in time (expect second order), for both
    translation and rotation.
  - Newton convergence: quadratic convergence of the residual norm
    (show the exact Jacobian pays off).
  - Monolithic vs converged partitioned on cases where both run: they
    should agree to the iteration tolerance.
  - Robustness: rigidBodyEndWhirl sweeps of moment and EI; the
    partitioned limit seen numerically, matching section 5.
  Sources: TR, tutorials/rigidBodyEnd*, rigidBodyAndBeam_loop and
  _beamFoamCoupled, the regression check of 2026-10-20 (agreement to
  ~2e-6 with identical Newton counts after the legacy removal).


8. Application: moored floating bodies in waves
-----------------------------------------------

  - floatRigidBody: floating box with a mooring line in waves; motion and
    line force, monolithic vs partitioned vs MoorDyn.
  - Slack line (floatRigidBody_slackLine): snap loads and compression,
    where line bending and ground contact matter.
  - Two lines (floatRigidBody_twoLine), if Phase 5 is complete.
  - Sweeps of line stiffness and wave period (floatRigidBody_sweeps):

  - Cost: Newton iterations per step (beamSolverIterations function

  Sources: tutorials/floatRigidBody*, plot_floatRigidBody_sweeps.py,
  plot_monolithic_vs_partitioned_*.py.


9. Discussion
-------------

  - When monolithic coupling matters (the stability number) and when
    partitioned is good enough.
  - Cost: the extra rows are cheap; the saving comes from fewer outer
    correctors and fewer failed runs.
  - Limitations:
      - one line per body today (Phase 5 plan for several lines);
      - the fluid stays partitioned (no fluid added mass in the
        monolithic system);
      - non-symmetric Jacobian, so a direct sparse LU; scaling to long
        lines and many lines;
      - geometric terms that are only first order in theta (T = I limit).
  - Extensions: several lines on one body, several bodies, implicit
    fluid added mass, iterative solvers with block preconditioners.
  Sources: P5, OC.
