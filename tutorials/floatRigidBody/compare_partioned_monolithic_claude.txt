floatRigidBody monolithic versus partitioned comparison
=======================================================

Cases compared
--------------

  floatRigidBody_monolithic           (nOuterCorrectors 3)
  floatRigidBody_partioned            (nOuterCorrectors 3)
  floatRigidBody_monolithic_nOuter8   (nOuterCorrectors 8)
  floatRigidBody_partioned_nOuter8    (nOuterCorrectors 8)

The directory name "partioned" is retained here because that is the existing
case name.

Scope and setup
---------------

The comparison isolates the rigid-body/mooring-line coupling method. The
initial fields, generated tank and beam meshes, beam properties, waves, CFD
schemes, PIMPLE settings and run scripts are identical. The only functional
input difference between monolithic and partitioned is the solver selected in
constant/dynamicMeshDict:

  Monolithic:  type beamFoamCoupled;
  Partitioned: type FvBeamNewmark;

The nOuter8 cases are copies of the corresponding base cases with only
nOuterCorrectors changed from 3 to 8 in system/fvSolution.

All cases use interFoam, moveMeshOuterCorrectors=yes, adaptive time stepping
with maxDeltaT=0.002 s, and the same motion constraints. Motion is restricted
to surge, heave and pitch; sway, roll and yaw are constrained.

"Monolithic" refers only to the box--mooring-line subsystem. Fluid and
structure are still coupled through the PIMPLE outer-corrector loop.

Coupling algorithms
-------------------

In the monolithic case, beamFoamCoupled passes the fluid force, moment and
motion constraints into beamFoam. The mooring line and rigid-body-end degrees
of freedom are solved together in the BlockEigen/Newton system, and moorFV
accepts the body state returned by beamFoam. The accelerationRelaxation=0.4
entry remains in the dictionary for comparison but is not used.

In the partitioned case, the current rigid-body motion prescribes the beam
attachment kinematics. beamFoam evolves the line and returns its reaction;
FvBeamNewmark then updates the body. accelerationRelaxation=0.4 relaxes the
acceleration between coupling iterations.

Completed 10 s results
----------------------

All four cases completed stably to 10 s with no beam Newton failures. Sway,
roll and yaw are exactly zero in every motion history, confirming that the
plane and axis constraints are applied by both coupling methods.

Outer-corrector convergence
---------------------------

Increasing nOuterCorrectors from 3 to 8 reduces the monolithic--partitioned
surge discrepancy by about a factor of ten (final 13.0 mm -> 1.3 mm; RMS after
6 s 11.4 mm -> 1.3 mm), with similar reductions in heave and pitch. The large
discrepancy at three outer correctors is therefore predominantly
outer-iteration (coupling) error rather than a genuine difference between
the converged solutions of the two methods.

The two methods do not converge at the same rate. Relative to its own 8
corrector run, the 3 corrector monolithic final surge differs by 2.2 mm,
whereas the 3 corrector partitioned final surge differs by 9.5 mm. Measured
against the other method's 8 corrector run, the differences are 3.5 mm
(Mono 3 vs Part 8) and 10.8 mm (Part 3 vs Mono 8). For the slow drift,
monolithic coupling at three outer correctors is therefore substantially
closer to the converged response than partitioned coupling at three.

This advantage does not extend to the initial transient. During the first
~1.5 s the 3 corrector monolithic run departs further from its 8 corrector
reference than the partitioned run does: maximum pitch difference 1.25 deg
versus 0.60 deg, and maximum attachment-force difference 0.086 N versus
0.056 N (see below).

Mooring loads
-------------

Attachment tension peak:

  Mono 3:   0.09212 N at 1.230 s
  Part 3:   0.09636 N at 1.206 s
  Mono 8:   0.09130 N at 1.154 s
  Part 8:   0.09478 N at 1.172 s

At three outer correctors the partitioned peak is about 4.6% larger and
occurs 0.024 s earlier than the monolithic peak. At eight outer correctors
both peaks move earlier, and the partitioned peak remains about 3.8% larger.
The peak timing shifts by 0.076 s between Mono 3 and Mono 8 and by 0.034 s
between Part 3 and Part 8.

Maximum instantaneous difference between the interpolated attachment-force
vectors:

  Part 3 - Mono 3:   0.04289 N near 1.154 s
  Part 8 - Mono 8:   0.03014 N near 1.084 s
  Mono 3 - Mono 8:   0.08624 N near 1.140 s
  Part 3 - Part 8:   0.05579 N near 1.124 s

The peak-tension transient is not yet converged in outer correctors for
either method, and a ~4% peak difference between the methods persists at
eight correctors.

The final tension magnitudes agree to 0.6% at three correctors (0.7550 vs
0.7508 mN) but differ by 3.2% at eight correctors (0.7589 vs 0.7347 mN). The
close agreement at three correctors is therefore partly coincidental, and the
final tension is not a reliable convergence indicator for this case.

Mooring-line shape
------------------

Although body motion and line tension agree closely, the mooring-line
configurations computed by the two methods are completely different. This is
visible in ParaView and in the beam fields (<time>/beamone/W, pointW, Theta).
All four cases start from identical beam fields at t=0.

In-plane shape. The line is initially almost taut, hanging 3.2 mm below the
anchor--attachment chord. As the box drifts towards the anchor the chord
shortens, the line becomes slack and it buckles. The two methods buckle in
opposite directions. Signed maximum offset of the line centreline from the
chord in the x-z plane (+ = above the chord):

  t (s)      0      0.3     0.5     1       2       5       10
  Mono 3   -3.2    +1.1    -8.4   -16.1   -46.5   -65.0   -65.8  mm
  Part 3   -3.2    +0.6    +7.1   +14.2   +48.0   +67.2   +69.8  mm
  Mono 8   -3.2    -2.6    -5.5    -9.1   -47.7   -65.5   -66.5  mm
  Part 8   -3.2    +2.1    +6.2   +11.2   +47.9   +65.8   +66.9  mm

The branch is selected between about 0.3 and 0.5 s and is independent of the
number of outer correctors: monolithic always bows downward, partitioned
always bows upward. The partitioned line leaves the anchor travelling up and
back towards x~0.235 m and arches over to the box; the monolithic line leaves
the anchor travelling forward and down and curves up to the box. At 10 s the
mid-span centrelines differ by about 108 mm in x and 90 mm in z.

Ground penetration. Because groundContactActive is false, the downward-bowed
monolithic line passes below the tank floor, reaching z ~ -4.8 mm in both
Mono 3 and Mono 8. The upward-bowed partitioned line never goes below z=0.

Twist and out-of-plane motion. In both partitioned runs the line remains
exactly planar: Theta_x and Theta_z remain ~1e-11 rad and W_y ~1e-13 m
throughout. In the monolithic runs a twist mode grows exponentially from
round-off: max |Theta_x| rises from 1.7e-11 rad at 0.40 s to 6.1e-8 at
0.50 s, 5.1e-3 at 0.60 s and 2.3 rad at 0.65 s (Mono 3; the growth occurs
about 0.1 s earlier in Mono 8). Thereafter the whole line carries a rotation
of magnitude ~pi about an axis of approximately (0.85, 0, 0.53), i.e. close
to the line chord. For a circular cross-section this is mainly an invisible
twist, but it is accompanied by an intermittent out-of-plane excursion of
the centreline of up to about +/-6 mm in y (largest near 2 s and 7 s).
Body sway, roll and yaw are identically zero and the loading is symmetric
in y, so this out-of-plane response has no physical driver.

Assessment. Neither line solution is credible:

  - Partitioned: a slack line with positive submerged weight (rho=141.8
    kg/m3) sustaining a ~70 mm arch above its chord against gravity is
    suspect. Rigid rotation of the line about its chord changes no strain
    energy, so the upward branch should be unstable to out-of-plane
    perturbations; it is likely preserved only because the partitioned
    solution remains exactly symmetric in y.
  - Monolithic: the downward bow is consistent with gravity, but the line
    penetrates the floor, and the twist/out-of-plane mode grows from
    round-off without forcing. This points to an issue in the monolithic
    beam/rigidBodyEnd coupling, possibly in the treatment of the end
    rotations. The source has not yet been inspected.

Because line tension is small (peak < 0.1 N), these very different line
configurations have little effect on the body motion. The convergence of the
two methods noted above therefore applies to body motion and line tension
only; the line shapes do not converge between methods.

At three outer correctors the monolithic run is 0.78% slower by execution
time and uses about 7.9% more beam Newton iterations per time step. At eight
outer correctors the Newton-iteration overhead rises to about 31%, but
execution times are identical to within 0.1% because the CFD solution
dominates the cost. Moving from three to eight outer correctors increases the
cost by about 2.5x for both methods.

Interpretation and limitations
------------------------------

At three outer correctors the two methods are not numerically equivalent,
but the 8 corrector runs show that they converge towards a common solution
as the outer-corrector count increases. For the slow drift, monolithic
coupling at three correctors is much closer to the converged response than
partitioned coupling at three, at the same computational cost. For the early
peak-tension transient neither method is converged at three correctors, and
the monolithic run is not more accurate there.

These conclusions concern body motion and line loads only. The mooring-line
shapes differ fundamentally between the methods (see "Mooring-line shape"),
and neither line solution is currently credible. Suggested follow-up:
repeat both methods with groundContactActive true; review the end-rotation
treatment in beamFoamCoupled/rigidBodyEnd; and seed a small out-of-plane
perturbation in the partitioned case to test the stability of its upward
branch.

The 8 corrector runs are not a fully converged reference: Part 8 and Mono 8
still differ by 1.3 mm in final surge, 3.8% in peak tension and 3.2% in
final tension. A further outer-corrector and/or time-step refinement would
be required to establish a converged reference for the transient loads.

beamFoamCoupled also has implementation restrictions relevant beyond this
specific one-line case: it supports one finiteVolumeBeam restraint, requires
the centre of rotation to coincide with the centre of mass, and currently
rejects restart from a displaced body state. The partitioned README restart
procedure should therefore not be assumed to work for the monolithic case.

Documentation correction
------------------------

The case READMEs previously described the box as 0.09 kg. All
dynamicMeshDict files use mass=0.124319 kg. The README values have been
corrected to match the inputs.

Data sources
------------

The results above were calculated from:

  postProcessing/sixDoF_History/0/sixDoFRigidBodyStateFvBeam.dat
  postProcessing/0/attachmentForcebeamone.dat
  postProcessing/interfaceHeight1/0/height.dat
  log.interFoam
  <time>/beamone/W, pointW, Theta
  constant/beamone/polyMesh/points

for all four cases. Line centrelines were obtained by adding pointW to the
beam mesh points and averaging each 32-point cross-section (41 sections). Surge and heave are centre-of-mass displacements from
(0.5, 0.025, 0.15) m; pitch is the y rotation component. Force histories
contain one entry per PIMPLE outer corrector; the last entry at each physical
time was retained. Histories with different adaptive time grids were compared
by linear interpolation. Beam Newton iterations are the sums of the
"N: Converged" counts in log.interFoam.
